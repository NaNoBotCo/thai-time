Telling Time in Thai

Thailand runs two clocks at once: the 24-hour clock on paper, and in speech an older count of six-hour blocks, named for a gong by day and a drum by night.

Six-hour clock

Two clocks at once

Written down, Thai time runs on the 24-hour clock: 14.30 น., read sip-si nalika sam-sip nathi. Said out loud it mostly runs on an older count: บ่ายสองโมงครึ่ง, bai song mong khrueng, “afternoon, two gongs, half”.

The spoken count cuts the day into four blocks of six hours and starts again at one in each block. Each block has its own word, and the words are sounds. โมง mong is the boom of a gong, struck through the day. ทุ่ม thum is the thud of a drum, struck through the evening. ตี ti, “strike”, takes the small hours.

So 2 a.m. is ตีสอง, 8 a.m. is แปดโมง, 2 p.m. is บ่ายสองโมง and 8 p.m. is สองทุ่ม. Pick the wrong block and you turn up six hours early, or twelve hours late.

Where the words come from

Gong by day, drum by night

Before clocks, the hours came from a tower. Keepers struck a gong (ฆ้อง khong) through the day and beat a drum (กลอง klong) through the night, once for each hour into the block. People named the hour by what they heard: โมง for the gong, ทุ่ม for the drum.

At 6 a.m., noon and 6 p.m. the keepers beat a long run of strokes, ย่ำ yam: dawn was ย่ำรุ่ง, noon ย่ำเที่ยง, dusk ย่ำค่ำ. In Ayutthaya the big drum at the foot of the drum tower was beaten at sunset to close the city gates.

Water clock

The coconut that sinks

นาฬิกา nalika, now the word for a clock, first meant a coconut. Siam kept time with a กะลาลอย kala loi: half a coconut shell with a small hole in the bottom, floated in a basin. Water seeps in, the shell rides lower, and when it is full it sinks. One sinking, one hour.

Keepers scored the shell with nine lines into ten parts, each a บาท bat of six minutes. In the palace clock house, each time the shell sank a keeper struck the gong once and pushed a tally stick onto a rail. Twelve sticks made a day or a night; the rail was cleared at dawn and at dusk.

บาท–
Minutes–
Sticks on the rail–

This shell is a half sphere, and it floats, so it rides lower as it fills. Water pushes in through the hole faster the higher the level outside stands above the level inside (Torricelli's law: the speed grows with the square root of that drop). The gap narrows as the bowl widens, so the shell fills more slowly toward the end; the nine lines here sit at equal times under that rule. The shell sinks when its rim reaches the water, before it is full.

King Mongkut (Rama IV) grumbled that the keepers guessed, calling ten บาท to the hour whatever the season, “…คงให้ได้ว่าความแต่ว่าโมงละ ๑๐ บาทอยู่นั้นเอง…”, and that visitors carrying pocket watches would laugh at hours struck short and long.

Royal Gazette

The king's list, 1900

On 23 July 1900 King Chulalongkorn (Rama V) had the hour names printed in the Royal Gazette. People had drifted, it said, and were saying 6 โมงเช้า, 6 โมงเย็น, 3 ทุ่ม, 6 ทุ่ม and 9 ทุ่ม. The list set them right.

In the king's list the drum count runs all night, up to eleven: 1 a.m. is เจ็ดทุ่ม, seven drums. Three points of the night get their own names: ยามหนึ่ง at 9 p.m., สองยาม at midnight, สามยาม at 3 a.m. A ยาม yam is a watch of three hours.

Today people say all five of the forms the list struck out, and ตี has taken the small hours from the drum.

The north

Lanna time: watches, not hours

The old Lanna count has no 60-minute hour. Inscriptions cut the day into sixteen watches, ยาม yam, of an hour and a half: eight by day and eight by night, from 6 a.m. A watch takes its name from the instrument that signalled it, then the part of the day. ตูด tut is a buffalo horn blown; กลอง klong the drum; แตร trae a horn or trumpet.

ยามกลองงาย, the morning-meal drum, runs from 7.30 to 9 a.m. The four watches found most in Lanna inscriptions all fall between dawn and early afternoon: ยามตูดเช้า, ยามกลองงาย, ยามแตรใกล้เที่ยง and ยามตูดซ้าย. Kannika Wimonkasem, who listed them, reads that as the hours chosen for ceremonies.

Still in the calendar

Lanna calendars still time the big moments by watch. The 2022 Lanna calendar compiled by Saknarin Chaongio at Rajamangala University of Technology Lanna puts Songkran in ยามแตรสู่เที่ยง, the horn watch toward noon, at 9.52.12 a.m. on 14 April, and the turn of the era year in ยามกลองแลง, the late-day drum, at 1.49.48 p.m. on 16 April. Both clock times fall inside the watches in the table above. แลง laeng is the northern word for late afternoon.

Five hundred years earlier, in 1502, an inscription at Wat Doi Kham in Lamphun dates its horoscope ยามแตรจักใกล้เที่ยง, “the horn watch, near noon”.

Sixty named days

Lanna also names each day from a cycle of sixty, the วันไท wan thai, “Tai day”. Ten first names and twelve last names turn together, one step a day, like two gears: กาบยี, ดับเหม้า, รวายสี, เมืองใส้ … The gears line up again after 60 days.

Both gears step at once, and 10 and 12 are both even, so a first name only ever meets every other last name: 60 of the 120 possible pairs occur. The years turn on the same two gears; the era year 1388, which began in April 2026, is ปีรวายสะง้า, a horse year.

Months two ahead

Lanna numbers its lunar months two ahead of central Thailand. Yi Peng, ยี่เป็ง, is the full moon of the second month in the north and of the twelfth in Bangkok. Tai Lue and Tai Khuen towns, Mae Sai, Chiang Khong, Chiang Kham and northern Nan among them, count เดือนโหรา duean hora, one month ahead of Bangkok and one behind the rest of Lanna.

Sun time

Noon by the sun

Thai clocks run on the 105th meridian east, the line through Ubon Ratchathani. Until 1 April 1920 Siam kept Bangkok's own sun time, 6 hours 42 minutes 4 seconds ahead of Greenwich; that night the clocks jumped 17 minutes 56 seconds.

Chiang Mai sits six degrees west of the line, so its sun peaks about 24 minutes after clock noon, more or less with the season. Pick a town.

In the northern forests people read the hour off the sun by hand: arm out, fingers spread, count hand-spans (คืบ khuep) up from the horizon. One span, one โมง; six spans, noon.

History

From drum tower to radio

  1. AyutthayaA drum tower ten wa high, four storeys, held three drums: ย่ำพระสุรศรี at the foot, beaten at sunset to close the gates; อัคคีพินาศ in the middle for fire; พิฆาตไพรินทร์ at the top for an enemy at the walls.
  2. Rama ITrials by diving were timed with a sinking coconut. The king worried over a shell that sank before either diver came up, and had a gong signal used instead.
  3. Rama IISmall watches arrived as Portuguese gifts. With no shirt pockets, people tucked them into the fold of the waist-cloth, the พก phok: so a pocket watch is นาฬิกาพก.
  4. Rama IVKing Mongkut set up a clock office in the palace, compared its pendulum clocks against the sundial at noon, and had the hour names announced in the Royal Gazette.
  5. 1889The solar calendar becomes the government calendar, the year starting on 1 April.
  6. 1900Rama V's announcement ประกาศใช้ทุ่มโมงยาม lists the hour names and strikes out five common mistakes.
  7. Noon gunThe navy fired a gun at noon so the city could set its clocks; later the power station blinked the lights at สองทุ่ม instead.
  8. 1920On 1 April Siam moves from Bangkok's sun time to the 105th meridian, UTC+7.
  9. TodayThe Royal Thai Navy's Hydrographic Department keeps Thailand's standard time; radio and TV read it at 08:00 and 18:00 with the anthem.
